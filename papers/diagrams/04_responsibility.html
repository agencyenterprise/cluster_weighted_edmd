E-Step: How Responsibilities $r_{ik}$ Are Computed
$r_{ik} = \dfrac{\pi_k \cdot \mathcal{N}(x_i;\, c_k, \Sigma_k) \cdot \mathcal{N}(\varepsilon_k;\, 0,\, \sigma_k^2 I)}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N} \pi_j \cdot \mathcal{N}(x_i;\, c_j, \Sigma_j) \cdot \mathcal{N}(\varepsilon_j;\, 0,\, \sigma_j^2 I)}$
Prior
$\pi_k$
How large is cluster $k$?
×
Proximity
$\mathcal{N}(x_i;\, c_k, \Sigma_k)$
How close is $x_i$ to center $c_k$?
×
Residual
$\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon_k;\, 0,\, \sigma_k^2 I)$
How well does $L_k$ predict $f(x_i)$?
Example: point $x_i$ scored against 3 clusters
Cluster 1 close + good $L_1$
$r_{i1}=0.95$
Cluster 2 far + ok $L_2$
$r_{i2}=0.05$
Cluster 3 medium + bad $L_3$
$r_{i3}=0.00$
In standard GMM, only the proximity factor exists.
The residual factor is what makes the clustering dynamics-aware:
cluster 3 is closer than cluster 2, but its local model predicts $f(x_i)$ badly → rejected.
