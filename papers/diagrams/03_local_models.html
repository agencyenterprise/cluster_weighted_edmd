Local-Model Variants ($L_k$)
A
physics
Taylor-Analytic
$L_k(x) = f(c_k) + J(c_k)(x - c_k)$
Params/cluster: 0 (uses analytic $f$, $J$)
Integrator: Euler
Requires: known ODE + Jacobian
Best for: physics-known, fine $N$
B
data-driven
Taylor-LS (Hybrid)
$L_k(x) = f_k + J_k(x - c_k)$
Params/cluster: $d + d^2$
Fit: weighted LS on $(x_i, f_i)$
Integrator: Euler
Requires: vector-field observations
C
data-driven
Local Continuous EDMD
$L_k(x) = [M_k \cdot \Phi(x - c_k)]_{1:d}$
Params/cluster: $\binom{d+p}{p}^2$
Fit: weighted LS: $\nabla\Phi \cdot f \approx M_k \cdot \Phi$
Integrator: Euler ($x_{t+1} = x_t + \Delta t \cdot L_k$)
Requires: vector-field observations
D
best on Lorenz
Local Discrete EDMD
$\hat{x}_{t+1} = c_k + [K_k \cdot \Phi(x_t - c_k)]_{1:d}$
Params/cluster: $\binom{d+p}{p}^2$
Fit: weighted lstsq: $\Phi(x_{t+1}) \approx K_k \cdot \Phi(x_t)$
Integrator: none (direct prediction)
Requires: consecutive state pairs only
All variants share the same E-step (Bayes' theorem with proximity + residual factors).
They differ only in the M-step: how $L_k$ is parameterized and fit from responsibility-weighted data.
